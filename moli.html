M·O·L·I

Mercy On Lost Individuals

MOLI is being built to respond to, record, and ultimately reduce the number of animals dying on UK roads — because right now, almost none of it is treated as a welfare issue at all.

The bit nobody’s job it is to care about

When an animal is killed on a UK road, there is usually no dedicated, humane, timely response. It may be swept aside by a highways contractor, left to decompose, or simply never recorded. There is no single body responsible for treating it as a welfare matter — most of what exists is a patchwork of maintenance duties, and maintenance isn’t mercy.

The gap between what gets reported and what actually happens is the clearest sign of how invisible this problem has been allowed to stay.

Other published estimates include on the order of ~50,000 badgers and tens of thousands of deer annually. Official logged removals on the strategic network capture only a fraction of the true toll.

What MOLI actually does

Three things, in order — because you can’t fix what you don’t first see, and you can’t prevent what you haven’t understood.

01 · Respond

Respond

A reporting line and volunteer network so an injured or killed animal on the road gets a fast, humane response — instead of being left, or treated purely as a hazard to clear.

02 · Record

Record

Every response is logged — species, location, road, time. Data that barely exists today, and that turns individual losses into evidence.

03 · Prevent

Prevent

That evidence is used to push for the things that actually reduce deaths — wildlife crossings, signage, speed and design changes — at the roads where it matters most.

“Every roadside death is an individual — not a statistic, not a hazard to clear. MOLI is mercy for the ones nobody stopped for.” — Founding note, MOLI
